Roaming and bill shock

A home-carrier roaming plan, including a daily "travel pass", bills you for using your normal SIM abroad. The pass is convenient and easy to start by accident.

How the bill grows

Carriers publish their own rates, and those rates change. This page does not quote them. The pattern is stable even when the numbers are not.

  • Data roaming left on. The phone joins a local network and treats it as your home plan's roaming. One toggle does that.
  • Background updates and photo or cloud backups. The phone can upload a burst of photos, or refresh apps, without you opening anything. That traffic counts.
  • Auto-play video. A social app or a browser tab that starts a video will use the pass, or the out-of-bundle rate, in the background of a conversation.
  • A daily pass charged per day, including light use. Some passes start for the day as soon as the phone uses a little data, then bill that day even if you only loaded a map for a minute.
  • Out-of-bundle rates after the pass runs out. When the included data for the day or the trip is gone, later use can fall onto a much higher rate. The phone does not always make that obvious.
  • Calls, and voicemail forwarding, while you are abroad. Answering a call can be charged as roaming. So can a call you never take, if the network forwards it to voicemail or to another number and your carrier bills that forward.
  • Plane and ship networks. Inflight and maritime networks are often billed as roaming, sometimes outside a normal travel pass. Wifi that still shows cellular bars can be one of these networks. Switching the phone to airplane mode, then turning wifi back on, avoids the cellular path.

How to avoid it

  • Turn data roaming off on the home line before you fly. On an iPhone that setting is per line under Settings, Cellular (or Mobile Service). On Android it is under the SIM or mobile-network settings, and the label varies.
  • Set the home line to calls and SMS only, or make it the secondary line, once you have a travel plan for data. Point mobile data at the eSIM or the local SIM.
  • Turn on Low Data Mode (iPhone) or Data Saver (Android) so backups and auto-updates are less eager. Also pause photo backup for the trip in the cloud app itself. Low Data Mode is a preference, not a spending cap.
  • Ask your carrier for a spending cap or a roaming bar before you leave. The name of that control is different at each carrier. Set it while you can still call them on your home network.
  • Read your carrier's current roaming page before you leave. If you do want their pass, know what starts it, whether a day is a calendar day, and what happens when the allowance is used up.

The habit that prevents the surprise is the same one in the site's quick recommendation: an eSIM or a local SIM for data, and home roaming off.

Get an eSIM before you fly

Buy the plan on wifi at home, install it, and leave that line off until you land. Keep data roaming off on your home SIM so a background update cannot start a travel pass.
